\begin{abstract}
We compare LoRA with an activation-dependent symmetric quadratic adapter at
matched parameter counts in frozen GPT-2. An independent 24-run WikiText-2
confirmation favors Symmetric in mean final validation and held-out loss at
ranks 1, 2, 4, and 8, whereas six controlled AG News runs favor LoRA in mean
accuracy and macro-F1. A single-seed scaling ablation, convergence curves,
local-Jacobian spectra, adapter Hessians, and a matched cost benchmark show that
the result is task and protocol dependent rather than universal.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning adapts a pretrained model while keeping its
backbone frozen. We ask whether a quadratic function of a projection input can
offer useful capacity at the same parameter count as a LoRA branch. This is an
independently evaluated formulation and controlled experimental study, not a
claim that quadratic low-rank networks are unprecedented.

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
The methods below are compared at the mathematical and architectural levels.
Published benchmark results obtained with different backbones, datasets,
training budgets, or evaluation protocols are not numerically ranked against
the experiments in this study.

LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} learns the low-rank linear correction
$\frac{\alpha}{r}(xA)B$ and is the direct experimental baseline. DoRA
\cite{liu2024dora} decomposes weight magnitude and direction. MoRA
\cite{jiang2024mora} uses a square trainable matrix with nonparametric
transforms and is designed to increase effective update rank, while HiRA
\cite{huang2025hira} uses a Hadamard-product high-rank weight update. LoRAN
\cite{li2024loran} applies a nonlinear transformation to the low-rank weight
update. These are contextual weight- or parameter-space methods and were not
experimentally reproduced here.

\subsection{PERA}
PERA~\cite{zhang2026pera} introduces polynomial expansion in parameter and
low-rank-factor space before factor composition. This study instead applies
the nonlinearity directly to projected activations:
\[
x\longrightarrow xU\longrightarrow (xU)\odot(xU)\longrightarrow P.
\]
Both approaches investigate higher-order structure in parameter-efficient
adaptation, but the polynomial or quadratic operation acts on different
mathematical objects. PERA was not used as an experimental baseline.

\subsection{QuadraNet V2}
For one output coordinate of the Symmetric adapter,
\[
\Delta y_j=xQ_jx^T,
\qquad
Q_j=\frac{\alpha}{r}U\,\operatorname{diag}(P_{:,j})\,U^T.
\]
This places the method within the family of factorized quadratic
transformations represented by work such as QuadraNet V2
\cite{xu2026quadranet}. Across a complete multi-output layer, all output
coordinates share the projection directions contained in $U$, while $P$
provides signed output-specific coefficients. Consequently, similarity at the
single-output quadratic-form level does not establish complete architectural
equivalence. QuadraNet V2 is treated as a closely related quadratic precedent,
not evidence of either exact equivalence or universal novelty.

\subsection{Position of the proposed method}
The defining feature of the Symmetric adapter is that its low-rank projection
is applied to the incoming activation and the projected coordinates are
squared before being mapped back to output space:
\[
\Delta y_{\mathrm{Symmetric}}
=\frac{\alpha}{r}\left((xU)\odot(xU)\right)P.
\]
This produces an activation-dependent quadratic correction with an
input-dependent Jacobian while retaining a parameter structure comparable in
size to the matched LoRA branch. The experiments evaluate this design directly
against LoRA under controlled budgets. DoRA, MoRA, HiRA, LoRAN, PERA, and
QuadraNet V2 clarify the literature context and are not reproduced baselines.

\section{Method}
The adapter is trained beside a frozen GPT-2 projection rather than replacing
the pretrained model. Only the low-rank branch is optimized, allowing the
functional form to be compared under matched trainable matrix shapes and
parameter counts. For a row vector $x\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times d}$, frozen
$W\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{out}}$, and frozen bias $b$, the base projection is
$y_{base}=xW+b$. LoRA and Symmetric add
\begin{align}
\Delta y_L &= \frac{\alpha}{r}(xA)B,\\
\Delta y_S &= \frac{\alpha}{r}((xU)\odot(xU))P.
\end{align}
Here $A,U\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}$ and
$B,P\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d_{out}}$. Both methods contain
$r(d+d_{out})$ trainable adapter parameters. For output $j$,
\begin{equation}
\Delta y_{S,j}=xQ_jx^T,\qquad
Q_j=\frac{\alpha}{r}U\operatorname{diag}(P_{:,j})U^T,
\end{equation}
so $Q_j$ is symmetric and $\operatorname{rank}(Q_j)\le r$. The directions in
$U$ are shared across outputs; signed coefficients in $P$ mean that $Q_j$ need
not be positive semidefinite. The expansion
$(ax_1+bx_2)^2=a^2x_1^2+2abx_1x_2+b^2x_2^2$ shows that the square creates
pairwise cross terms. LoRA remains linear in its input, whereas Symmetric
introduces explicit second-order interactions.

The controlled rank study uses $\alpha=4$, so $\alpha/r$ equals 4, 2, 1, and
0.5 at ranks 1, 2, 4, and 8. The separate constant-scale ablation was motivated
by this coupling of rank and effective scale.

\section{Experiments}
The work separates exploratory campaigns from controlled experiments. Early
studies compared adapter families, training durations, attention and MLP
placements, a full 12-block attention scan, and multilayer fixed-budget designs.
Their protocols were not uniform, so they are not merged with controlled data.

Controlled WikiText-2 experiments use frozen GPT-2 Small, consecutive
128-token causal-language-model blocks, and one adapter beside zero-based block
0 \texttt{attn.c\_proj}. Frozen projection and adapter outputs are summed before
attention residual dropout and residual addition. Runs use batch size 1,
gradient accumulation 4, AdamW at $3\times10^{-4}$, gradient clipping 1.0, no
scheduler, ranks 1/2/4/8, $\alpha=4$, and seeds 42/123/456. The independent
1,000-step screening and 5,000-step confirmation are not concatenated.

Best recorded validation, final validation at the declared step, and held-out
test evaluation of the final checkpoint are distinct. Test data do not select
the method, rank, hyperparameters, or checkpoint.

AG News uses the same frozen backbone and placement with a trainable
classification head, 4,096 training examples, a fixed 1,000-example stratified
validation subset, the original test split, 500 optimizer steps, paired seeds,
and 9,216 trainable parameters including the head. Matching backbone,
placement, rank, budget, optimizer, duration, and seed set limits obvious
confounds; separate scale, convergence, and downstream analyses address the
remaining questions.

The following derivative analyses characterize trained adapters; they do not
by themselves explain the observed performance differences.

\subsection{Local Jacobians}
LoRA and Symmetric have
\begin{align}
J_{\mathrm{LoRA}}&=\frac{\alpha}{r}AB,\\
J_{\mathrm{Sym}}(x)&=2\frac{\alpha}{r}U\operatorname{diag}(xU)P.
\end{align}
LoRA is linear at the adapter level, so its Jacobian is input independent and
can be represented by one fixed low-rank update matrix. Symmetric is quadratic;
its local transformation changes with $\operatorname{diag}(xU)$ and cannot in
general be reduced to one constant $\Delta W$. The analytical expressions were
checked against PyTorch autograd in double precision.

These quantities are not interchangeable notions of ``weight rank.'' In
particular, the input-dependent Symmetric local-Jacobian rank is not a direct
analogue of a fixed LoRA weight-update rank.

\subsection{Second-order structure}
For fixed linear output reduction $c$,
\begin{equation}
H=2\frac{\alpha}{r}U\operatorname{diag}(Pc)U^T.
\end{equation}
LoRA has an exactly zero adapter-only Hessian because its branch is linear.
Symmetric mean Hessian Frobenius norms are 155.15, 92.13, 52.68, and 17.61 at
ranks 1, 2, 4, and 8. The decrease combines learned-factor differences with
$\alpha/r$ scaling and is not evidence of weaker or less useful interactions
at higher rank. For fixed $c$, the Hessian is input independent, so repeated
token activations are not independent Hessian observations. Double-precision
autograd tests verify the formula. A full Transformer-block Hessian was not
evaluated and is not reported as an empirical result.

\section{Conclusion}
This study investigated whether a simple quadratic transformation in a
low-dimensional activation space can provide a useful alternative to
conventional linear low-rank adaptation. The results show that the Symmetric
Quadratic Adapter is a practical and computationally lightweight mechanism for
introducing explicit second-order interactions into a frozen Transformer while
retaining the same low-rank parameter structure used for comparison with LoRA.

The strongest evidence comes from the controlled WikiText-2 experiments.
Across 24 independent 5,000-step runs covering ranks 1, 2, 4, and 8 and three
random seeds, Symmetric achieved lower mean final validation loss and lower
mean held-out test loss than LoRA at every tested rank. The largest final-test
difference was at rank 8, where mean loss decreased from
$3.7319\pm0.0035$ for LoRA to $3.6938\pm0.0044$ for Symmetric. The rank-1
trajectory also depends on optimization time: LoRA initially performs better,
while Symmetric overtakes it between the recorded 2,000- and 3,000-step
checkpoints. Rank and duration therefore matter in addition to final parameter
budget.

Holding $\alpha/r$ constant changes the magnitude of the differences but, for
the tested seed, preserves the 1,000-step sign pattern: LoRA performs better at
rank 1, whereas Symmetric performs better at ranks 2, 4, and 8. This pattern is
not explained exclusively by the primary protocol's changing scale, but the
single-seed ablation prevents a broader conclusion.

AG News provides a counterexample to general superiority. LoRA achieved higher
mean test accuracy and macro-F1. Accuracy was $0.8201\pm0.0051$ for LoRA and
$0.8021\pm0.0240$ for Symmetric, with greater variability for the quadratic
adapter. The combined evidence indicates a task-dependent trade-off.

The structural analyses establish that the adapters are genuinely different.
LoRA is linear in its adapter input and has a fixed low-rank update. Symmetric
has an input-dependent local Jacobian and explicit non-zero adapter-level
second derivatives, so it cannot generally be reduced to one constant
$\Delta W$. Local-Jacobian effective rank, learned-factor rank, and
output-covariance rank are distinct quantities rather than interchangeable
notions of weight rank. These properties do not establish a causal explanation
for task performance.

The added structure has little measured cost in the matched rank-4 benchmark:
forward and backward latency are almost identical to LoRA, and peak memory
increases by about 1.25 MiB. Thus, in this implementation, explicit quadratic
activation interactions operate at approximately the same practical scale as
the matched linear adapter.

Taken together, \textbf{low-rank quadratic activation-space adaptation is a
viable design point for parameter-efficient Transformer adaptation, and under
the controlled WikiText-2 protocol it consistently improves final
language-model loss relative to matched LoRA.} The AG News result demonstrates
that this advantage does not automatically transfer across tasks. Functional
form therefore merits treatment as an experimental variable alongside rank and
parameter budget.

The evidence remains limited to GPT-2 Small, restricted placements, two
datasets, three seeds for the principal experiments, and one seed for the
scaling ablation. It establishes neither state-of-the-art performance nor
broad statistical significance, universal transfer, a causal link from
second-order structure to loss, or universal mathematical novelty. Replication
on larger models, additional tasks, broader placements, and independently
repeated scaling configurations is required to determine how broadly the
observed behavior generalizes.
